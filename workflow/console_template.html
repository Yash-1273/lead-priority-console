The idea

Rank leads by what they're likely to earn, not just whether they'll book.

When experts can't call everyone, the top of the queue should hold the most expected revenue. Every other lead still gets the AI agent or nurture.

Standard lead scoring treats a shared room in Leicester and a London studio as the same lead. At University Living they aren't. Commission follows the rent, and the student enquires on a specific property, so the value of a lead is known the moment it arrives. Extras like flights, money transfer and guarantor are earned per student, so they count too.

Browsing behaviour does most of the work

AUC when a signal is removed. Browsing behaviour carries most of the weight.

Risk to check on real data: the dataset doesn't say when browsing time was measured. If it includes visits after the sales call, part of it is leakage. In production, count browsing up to the enquiry, then re-score as more arrives.

Better questions beat better algorithms

Adding the enquiry form's answers lifted the model more than any algorithm did.

So ask better questions. Three qualifying questions on University Living's enquiry form:

  • Do you have an offer letter or CAS yet?
  • Which intake are you moving for?
  • What's your weekly budget?

The data

What's real, what's simulated, what's assumed

Real
  • 9,240 leads with real booked / not booked outcomes (Kaggle, X Education), mapped to University Living channels
  • Rent levels: London en-suite ₹32,893 and studio ₹43,307 a week; UK average ₹19,431 and ₹28,956 (published 2025/26 surveys: £259, £341, £153, £228)
  • Every score on this page comes from the trained model
Simulated
  • The property each lead enquired on (city, room, tenancy, move-in date). It's drawn independently of the outcome, so no link between value and intent is invented.
  • Daily loss of uncalled leads in the 30-day test
Assumed
  • Commission rate (adjustable above)
  • Extras per booking: ₹15,000 expected from flights, money transfer, guarantor and essentials, blended across take-up (adjustable above)
  • Conversion rate comes from the proxy data (38.5%). University Living's raw enquiries likely convert lower, which stretches the team further and increases the gain from value ranking.
  • Lead volume: this is one team's slice of the day. What matters is capacity as a share of leads.
  • Exchange rate: ₹127 per £ (Oct–Nov 2026)
  • Manchester and Birmingham rents: midpoints of published ranges
  • With University Living's CRM data, all of these are replaced by real values. The method stays the same.

Built with AI: I used Claude to research University Living's business, audit the data, write the model and simulation, and build this console. The product decisions were mine: rank by value, keep post-call data out, test urgency and drop it when it failed, and match channel cost to lead value.
