Season-test gain at 25 calls a day

Extras are earned per student, whatever the rent, so they narrow the gap between cheap and expensive rooms. The season test was run at ₹0, ₹15,000 and ₹30,000; the gain holds at all three. Commission is fixed at 10% of rent: changing it scales every ₹ figure but barely changes who gets called.

What if students wanting expensive rooms book less often?

The test assigns each lead's property without any link to whether they booked. In reality, students enquiring about expensive rooms may be more price-sensitive, and the model wouldn't know. So I made high-value enquirers book less often and re-ran the 60-day test (25 calls a day):

Next: AI that reads the conversation

The biggest model gain came from better information, not a better algorithm. SABIE chats and AI voice-agent calls already contain it: an LLM can pull out CAS status, intake and weekly budget and add them to the score, with no extra form for the student.

Every student gets a first response within 24 hours, whether from an expert, the AI voice agent or WhatsApp. Lead Desk decides who gets the expensive channel first; it never decides who gets ignored.

How it works and what's real

Five steps, four real datasets

1 An enquiry arrives with its property attached → 2 only information known at that moment is used (post-call notes leak the outcome) → 3 the model gives the chance to book, lowered as the lead stays open → 4 × revenue value (rent commission + extras) × visa grant rate = priority → 5 engaged students keep their expert; the rest are ranked: the top get experts with a deadline, then the AI agent or nurture, re-scored on every action. After 5 unanswered calls, nurture. On rollout: merge duplicate enquiries from the same student.

Real
  • 9,240 leads with real booked / not booked outcomes (Kaggle, X Education), mapped to University Living channels
  • How long leads stay open: 8,000 leads with dates (Olist). Half of deals close within 14 days
  • When to stop calling: 41,188 customers called repeatedly (UCI bank). By the 5th call, a call converts at under half the rate of the 1st
  • Visa: about 96% of Indian students' UK study visas granted (Home Office, 2025). Rents: 2025/26 UK surveys
Simulated
  • The property each lead enquired on, drawn without any link to the outcome, so no pattern is invented. The break-even test shows how much price sensitivity it would take to cancel the gain
  • Today's follow-ups' call history, how many calls each booker needs, and how long they keep looking: drawn from the real bank and Olist patterns, and stress-tested with different ones
Assumed
  • Commission 10% of rent, extras ₹15,000 per booking (adjustable above), ₹127 per £
  • Engaged follow-ups (shortlist sent, deposit or visa stage) take a set number of expert calls; the datasets can't tell engaged from unresponsive leads, so it's a setting, not a model
  • The 38.5% booking rate comes from the proxy data. Real enquiries likely convert lower, which stretches the team and increases the gain
  • Leads with the AI agent or nurture count as not called in the season test, so the gain is conservative

Built with AI: I used Claude to research University Living's business, audit the data, write the model and simulations, and build this console. The product decisions were mine: rank by value, keep post-call data out, put new and follow-up leads in one plan, keep engaged students with their expert, and test my own ideas, dropping the ones that failed.
